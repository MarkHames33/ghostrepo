Getting started

What to expect when you contact a law office

September 12, 2026By Garcia Law Office

Contacting a law office is usually the first step toward understanding a legal concern. You do not need to explain everything using legal terms. A clear summary of what happened, who is involved, and what you need help with is enough to begin.

The office may ask for your name, contact details, a short description of the concern, and any important deadline. Depending on the facts, it may assist with consultation, document review, contracts, notarial services, property concerns, family matters, negotiation, or formal representation.

Keep relevant contracts, notices, receipts, titles, identification, messages, photographs, and other records together. Write down important dates and bring copies when possible. Fees and scope depend on the nature, complexity, and urgency of the service requested.

Information shared online is for initial coordination only and does not create an attorney-client relationship until the office formally accepts the engagement. This article is for general educational purposes and is not legal advice.

Contracts and agreements

Five things to review before signing a contract

September 10, 2026By Garcia Law Office

A contract can affect your money, property, work, or responsibilities long after it is signed. Before agreeing, identify the parties correctly and make sure each person or organization is legally described using the correct name and address.

Read the duties and deadlines carefully. Check what must be delivered, when payment is due, what happens when someone fails to perform, and whether the agreement contains penalties, interest, renewal terms, or a process for ending the relationship.

Look for terms that are unclear or inconsistent. Blank spaces, handwritten changes, missing attachments, and references to documents you have not received should be resolved before signing. Keep a complete, signed copy together with proof of payment and important communications.

Do not sign simply because the other party says the document is routine or urgent. Ask questions about provisions you do not understand and seek legal review when the agreement involves property, significant money, long commitments, or a possible dispute.

This article provides educational information and does not determine whether a particular contract is valid or enforceable.

Property and land

Starting a property concern with the right documents

September 8, 2026By Garcia Law Office

Property concerns are rarely resolved by one document alone. Ownership, boundaries, possession, inheritance, leases, taxes, and prior transactions may all form part of the same story. Begin by collecting the documents that show how the property came to be owned or occupied.

Useful records may include the transfer certificate of title, tax declaration, deed of sale, lease, survey plan, receipts, tax payments, barangay records, and written agreements with neighboring owners or occupants. Note which documents are originals and which are copies.

Prepare a simple history of the property. Include previous owners, dates of transfers, boundary changes, improvements, payments, and disagreements. Mark the location and describe the issue in practical terms, such as an encroachment, unpaid obligation, disputed sale, or missing document.

A lawyer can then identify which facts need verification and whether coordination, document review, negotiation, administrative action, or court proceedings may be appropriate. Avoid selling, signing, or surrendering possession while important ownership questions remain unresolved.

Property rules and procedures vary by situation. This article is a general starting point, not legal advice.

Notarial services

How to prepare a document for notarization

September 6, 2026By Garcia Law Office

Notarization confirms a person’s identity and the circumstances in which a document is signed. It does not automatically make an unfair, incomplete, or unlawful agreement valid. The document should be complete and its important terms should be understood before anyone signs.

Bring the original document and an acceptable government-issued identification card. The names on the document and identification should match. Depending on the document, witnesses or additional supporting records may also be required.

Do not sign in advance unless the notary specifically instructs you to do so. The signatory may need to sign or acknowledge the signature in the notary’s presence and answer questions confirming understanding and willingness.

For affidavits, authorizations, contracts, and similar documents, prepare the facts carefully and check names, dates, amounts, addresses, and property descriptions. Ask for corrections before signing. Keep the notarized original or your copy in a secure place after the transaction.

Requirements can differ depending on the document and the parties involved. Contact the office before visiting if you are unsure what to bring.
